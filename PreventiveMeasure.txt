Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Two-factor authentication adds an extra layer of security by requiring users to verify their identity through a secondary method, such as a code sent to their phone or email. Even if someone steals your password, they won’t be able to access your account without this second factor. Enabling 2FA is especially important for email, social media, and banking accounts.

Shared Sensitive Documents: Discarding documents with personal information, such as bank statements, medical records, or tax returns, can put you at risk of dumpster diving—a method thieves use to retrieve valuable information. Shredding these documents ensures that your details cannot be pieced together and used fraudulently.
 
Monitor Financial Statements: Regularly checking your bank, credit card, and payment app statements allows you to identify unauthorized transactions early. Many financial institutions also offer alerts for unusual activity, which you can enable for additional oversight. Promptly reporting suspicious charges can prevent further misuse of your accounts.

Use Secure Networks: Public Wi-Fi networks, such as those in cafes or libraries, are not always secure and can be exploited by hackers to intercept your data. Avoid performing sensitive tasks like online shopping or accessing bank accounts on public Wi-Fi. When using such networks, employ a Virtual Private Network (VPN) to encrypt your connection and protect your information.

Be Cautious with Apps: Apps can collect and misuse data without your knowledge. Always download apps from trusted sources, such as official app stores, and carefully review the permissions they request. If an app asks for access to data unrelated to its functionality (e.g., a calculator app requesting location access), it’s a red flag.

Report Suspicious Activity: If you notice any signs of phishing emails, hacking attempts, or fraudulent activity, report it immediately to a trusted adult, your school, or local authorities. For example, phishing scams often come in the form of fake emails or messages pretending to be from legitimate sources, asking for sensitive information like passwords or credit card numbers. By acting quickly, you can limit potential damage and prevent further issues.
